Overall, I am fascinated by how pathogens outsmart our immune system: understanding how single-celled African trypanosomes, defended only by a single glycoprotein surface, outwit our complex and multi-layered immune system is what gets me out of bed in the morning. In Nov 2026, I started a Wellcome Career Development Award to establish my lab at the University of York. I really enjoy working as a team to tackle research questions in the lab, and I am really excited to be part of the Biology department at York where we  are surrounded by like-minded colleagues and fantastic facilities. 
Bio: I fell into Biochemistry a little by accident - my very clever friend was applying so it seemed like a good bet.  Then, during my undergraduate at Cardiff University, I spent a year in an immunology lab (Zsuzsanna Tabi) where I was given a freedom to explore the lab beyond my expectations. I was hooked on research from then on. I then started to get to grips with molecular biology techniques in Dafydd Jones' and Mark Young's lab before starting my PhD in Carolyn Moores' lab at Birkbeck in London in 2015. There, I had a fantastic time learning cryo-electron microscopy and investigating the motor protein kinesin-5 from the malaria causing parasite. That experience committed me to cryo-EM as a technique for understanding molecular mechanism, and also to studying parasites, which generate huge socio-economic pressure globally, but also have evolved fascinating and often very divergent biology from which we can learn so much. In 2020, I joined Matt Higgins' lab in Oxford for a postdoc studying the host-pathogen interactions of African trypanosomes which was a really vibrant and enjoyable place to do a postdoc. We worked very closely with Mark Carrington's lab in Cambridge, and collectively, this helped me build the launchpad to setup my own lab in York.
